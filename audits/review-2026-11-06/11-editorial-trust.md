# Auditor 11 — Blog editorial trust

Reviewed 6 November 2026. Evidence: current frontend and the fresh 106-page crawl (`../seo-live-2026-11-06.json`), including all 20 English blog records and sampled article text. No application changes.

## Findings

1. **P1 editorial correction — Specific research attribution cannot be checked from the article.** [Why Every Child Should Learn Coding](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/why-every-child-should-learn-coding) attributes stronger logical reasoning among children starting at ages 6–9 to MIT Media Lab research. Its HTML has no research citation link, study title, author or year. This is an unsupported attribution on the page, not proof the research does not exist. Locate the actual primary study and describe its population and findings accurately; otherwise remove or qualify the claim. Acceptance: a reader can follow the source and verify the precise claim. None of the 20 English blog records includes a non-brand external reference link in the crawl; that alone is not a defect for every article, but makes checking research and tool-specific assertions harder.

2. **P2 — Team bylines do not identify the relevant expertise or review responsibility.** All 20 English BlogPosting records identify “StemTechLab Team” as Organization, which is preferable to inventing an individual author. However, `src/app/[locale]/blogs/[slug]/BlogDetailClient.tsx:79–89` renders only a plain name and publication date; `page.tsx:97` emits only author type/name. Add a link to a real editorial-team description or a consenting, qualified author/reviewer profile, explaining who checks educational claims. Existing About-page instructor bios are useful but do not establish who wrote or reviewed each article. Acceptance: each article has truthful, navigable attribution and a defined review owner.

3. **P2 — Firsthand teaching claims need product-specific confirmation.** [Five Benefits of Robotics](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/5-benefits-of-robotics-for-children) says children connect wires and work together in “our robotics classes”; its related current course describes virtual simulations. Those formats could coexist, so this is a verification gap rather than a demonstrated falsehood. Have the instructor confirm which experiences this course actually delivers, distinguish general robotics benefits from this provider's lessons, and add one permission-cleared real project example where available.

4. **P3 — Editorial revision date is not visible.** The BlogPosting and Open Graph use `updatedAt` as modification time (`page.tsx:46–47,98–99`), while the visible article displays publication date only (`BlogDetailClient.tsx:37–43,85–89`). The English records have publication dates in January–May and modification dates in August–September 2026. No false freshness is established. Confirm `updatedAt` reflects substantive editorial changes before exposing an accurate “Reviewed/updated” date and concise revision note; routine database activity should not imply renewed expert review.

## Guidance and limits

Google's [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) recommends clear sourcing, demonstrable expertise and accurate authorship. These are editorial improvements, not a guaranteed ranking or AI-citation mechanism. No plagiarism determination, author credential verification, underlying study verification, or search-performance attribution was performed. English article citation-link coverage was checked; Arabic editorial equivalence was not exhaustively reviewed here.
