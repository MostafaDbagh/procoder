# Auditor 10 — Blog content quality and originality

Reviewed 6 November 2026. Evidence: fresh `../seo-live-2026-11-06.json`; close reading of six English articles (Minecraft, competitions, starting age, robotics comparison, screen time, Arabic teaching) and three matching Arabic articles (Minecraft, starting age, Arabic teaching). These are sampled editorial findings, not a full fact-check of all 40 localized article URLs. No source changes.

## Findings

1. **P1 editorial priority — Articles promise services that the linked catalog does not substantiate.** [Minecraft](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/minecraft-coding-for-kids) advertises a dedicated Minecraft/Python track, but its related courses are general Python and Programming Foundations, with no Minecraft requirements or curriculum shown. [Arabic teaching](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/teaching-arabic-to-children-in-digital-age) describes StemTechLab Arabic-language instruction and native Arabic teachers, but supplies no corresponding course. Both claims recur in Arabic. This does not prove the services do not exist; it establishes a verification and conversion gap. Confirm the offers with operations, then link the actual course and specify availability, or revise the articles to accurately describe the current STEM service. Do not simply delete useful articles without checking their traffic and links.

2. **P2 — High-intent guides stop before the practical decision.** [Robotics vs coding](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/robotics-vs-coding-which-first) assigns interests to courses but omits device requirements, physical versus simulated robotics, cost/equipment differences and example first projects. [Minecraft](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/minecraft-coding-for-kids) omits supported edition, platform, licensing, setup and a demonstrated project. Add an instructor-tested example and a clear decision table. The defect is missing decision support, not article length; boilerplate and related-course descriptions must not be counted as article depth.

3. **P2 — Specific factual claims need sourcing and qualification.** [Starting age](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/best-age-to-start-coding) declares age six universally best without evidence, although readiness varies within the article's own discussion. [Competitions](https://www.stemtechlab.com/en/blogs/coding-competitions-for-kids) places Hour of Code under contests, provides no event/eligibility links, and promises a college-application boost without support. The current [official Hour of Code destination](https://hourofai.org/en-US) describes learning activities and an initiative, not a competitive event. Distinguish activities from competitions; verify named programs, local availability, eligibility and dates against official organizers. Present the age recommendation as an instructor's contextual advice, with readiness criteria rather than a universal rule.

4. **P2 — Limited firsthand evidence makes the sampled posts hard to cite distinctively.** The sampled articles use a team byline and broad advice but show no worked lesson, teacher observation, project screenshot, student outcome with consent, or source references. Add a named reviewer with relevant experience, a concrete lesson example and sources where claims require them. Preserve the clear headings, concise answers and relevant course links already present. [Google's content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) emphasizes original information, demonstrated expertise, completeness and trustworthy sourcing; it does not prescribe a word-count target.

## Limits

No plagiarism database or corpus comparison was run. Generic wording is not evidence of copying or AI authorship. No ranking penalty, traffic impact, or guaranteed AI citation improvement is established. Confirm business facts before publishing corrections. Arabic samples repeat the core factual gaps and additionally retain English category, tag and region labels; localization quality deserves its own review.
